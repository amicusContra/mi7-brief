## 1. THE "CONVICTED FELON" CAMPAIGN

### What Barrett Said

On August 1, 2026, Rep. Barrett publicly called his Democratic opponent a "convicted felon" during a press conference, referencing a 2013 arrest at an Enbridge pipeline protest in Ingham County.

> "He didn't just chain himself to a bulldozer; he tried to shut down a pipeline. I know you guys want to cover up for him and everything, but it's indisputable that he's a convicted felon."
> — Tom Barrett, as reported by [MLive](https://www.mlive.com/politics/2026/08/us-rep-tom-barrett-labels-opponent-convicted-felon-stemming-from-protest-arrest.html)

### What Actually Happened

- **2013:** Four individuals arrested at Enbridge construction site in Ingham County during a water-protection protest.
- **Charge:** Felony resisting and obstructing a police officer + misdemeanor trespassing.
- **Outcome:** Guilty plea.
- **Expungement:** The conviction was later **set aside** under Michigan's Clean Slate Act (MCL 780.621 et seq.).

Under Michigan law, an expunged conviction is treated "as though it did not occur" for most purposes (MCL 780.623). The Michigan State Police maintains a nonpublic record accessible only to courts, law enforcement, and prosecutors.

**Barrett knows the conviction was expunged.** His campaign chose to use the "convicted felon" label anyway.

### The Collateral Damage

Barrett's "convicted felon" messaging was amplified on social media, particularly Facebook. The language was not limited to his opponent — it was used broadly to characterize housing advocacy organizations and their volunteers.

**Impact on RITDH volunteers:** At least one volunteer with a prior felony conviction — who was contributing to the MI Rent Is Too Damn High Coalition — lost volunteer capacity after Barrett's smear campaign created a hostile environment around anyone with a criminal record in housing advocacy spaces.

This is the chilling effect. When a sitting congressman labels housing advocacy as criminal activity, every volunteer with any record becomes a liability. The people most affected by housing instability — those who have been through the criminal justice system — are pushed out of the organizations fighting for them.

### The Irony

Michigan's Clean Slate Act exists specifically because the legislature recognized that people who have served their sentences deserve the opportunity to rebuild. Research from Safe & Just Michigan found that people who successfully set aside a conviction saw **average wage gains of 23%** in the first year after their record was cleared.

Barrett is weaponizing the very records that Michigan law says should not be used this way.

## 3. THE ELECTION DATE AD (2024)

### What Happened

In October 2024, Barrett's campaign placed a full-page ad in the **Michigan Bulletin** — a Black-owned publication serving Lansing with approximately 30,000 readers.

The ad read: **"On November 6 VOTE FOR TOM BARRETT."**

Election Day was **November 5, 2024.**

### The Differential Treatment

The Michigan Legislative Black Caucus noted that Barrett's campaign ran a similar ad in a Lansing alternative weekly paper — **with the correct date.**

### The Response

- **Michigan Legislative Black Caucus:** Filed a formal complaint alleging voter suppression
- **Michigan Attorney General:** Issued a **cease-and-desist letter** to Barrett's campaign
- **Barrett's campaign (Jason Roe):** Called it "nothing but a proofing error"
- **Michigan Bulletin publisher Marcus Jefferson:** Said the campaign told him they used an old template from the August 6 primary and changed "August" to "November" without changing the "6"

**Sources:** [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2024/10/14/politics/michigan-tom-barrett-election-ad), [WILX](https://www.wilx.com/2024/10/15/misprinted-tom-barrett-ad-sparks-congress-race-controversy/), [ClickOnDetroit](https://www.clickondetroit.com/news/local/2024/10/15/black-voters-in-michigan-react-to-controversial-election-ad-error/)
